# Decision points

Include a choice only when different answers produce materially different
study designs. Good candidates are:

- a 5-point versus 7-point scale;
- one response versus checkbox selection;
- adding or excluding a meaningful contrast segment;
- keeping or removing expensive free-text follow-ups;
- using actual stimulus material versus an approved representation;
- quality-versus-cost model choice;
- a neutral versus intentionally framed condition;
- running a pilot before a costly or untested full design.

State the current choice, its benefit, and its cost. Avoid generic options such
as “change the model” or “modify the agents.” Do not manufacture three choices
for a simple, fully specified request; one approval option may be sufficient.
